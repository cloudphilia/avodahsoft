For the media desk

Built for a volunteer on a Sunday morning.

Hears the reference

“Turn with me to Romans eight twenty-eight” and the verse is queued before the page is found. A sure reference goes straight to the screens if you ask; the rest wait for one key. In the King James, or the translation named out loud.

Follows the reading

Once a passage is up, the verse being read lights up, the others fade, and the page turns itself. Cautious on purpose: it waits rather than jumps.

Catches a quote

“I can do all things through Christ…” with no chapter and verse still finds Philippians 4:13 — in the King James’ words or a modern Bible’s, a word misheard, or split by a breath.

Up to eight screens

The projector, the choir’s monitor, the lobby and the stream each take a look — a theme, a text size, which layers to show — and every screen sees the same service. The stream goes into OBS as a browser source.

Lyrics, slides and video

Lyrics pasted once and arranged by part, PowerPoint, Keynote and PDF decks, slides made in Lector, videos and pictures behind the words, walk-in music, and a loop of announcements for the foyer. The stage screen and a phone remote come along.

Plans ahead

Save an order of service under a name and a day, or drag one onto a calendar. On the morning, the Service column offers it: open, and the whole Sunday is waiting.

Stage and remote

On the stage, and in your pocket.

The stage screen on a display or an iPad on the music stand: the verse, what is next, the clock, the countdown and a word from the desk. The remote on any phone on the church’s Wi-Fi, unlocked with a PIN.

Why Lector

The verse should not be late.

Every church has watched a volunteer type a reference while the preacher moves on. Presentation software treats scripture as one more slide to prepare. Lector treats it as something that happens live: it hears the reference, finds the verse and offers it, and the operator’s job becomes one keystroke. When the preacher wanders off the plan, the screens still keep up.

The rest of the service is there too, in one running order: the songs, the announcements, the offering slides, the video. The stage screen shows the speaker the verse, what is next, the notes and a countdown — on a display, or an iPad on the music stand — and a phone remote or a Stream Deck drives it from anywhere in the room.

Privacy

Private by design. Nothing leaves the room.

  • Speech recognition runs on the Mac. The sermon is never sent anywhere to be understood.
  • Four Bibles are on the disk and work with no internet: the King James, the American Standard, the Berean Standard and the World English Bible. Licensed translations arrive a verse at a time.
  • The sermon write-up is off until the church turns it on, and the switch says plainly what leaves the computer when it does.
  • The phone remote and the stage display run over the church’s own Wi-Fi, with a PIN.
  • Lector backs itself up every day into Documents, and the work comes back on a new computer from one file.
  • No account, no analytics, no tracking. Nothing to sign up for.
